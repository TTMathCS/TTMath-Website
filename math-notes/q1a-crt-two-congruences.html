Problem 1(a)

Chinese Remainder Theorem · two small congruences

Warm-up riddle: I'm odd, and on a 3-hour clock I sit where 4 would land. Who am I? Two clues, one number — that's all CRT ever asks.
Solve.  x ≡ 1 (mod 2)  and  x ≡ 4 (mod 3).
Tool: CRT with small moduli — just test cases

The answer lives mod 6

The moduli 2 and 3 are coprime, so CRT promises one answer modulo 2 × 3 = 6.

Simplify the second condition

4 ≡ 1 (mod 3), so the system really asks for x that is odd and ≡ 1 (mod 3).

List and pick

Odd numbers: 1, 3, 5, 7, … Of these, which are ≡ 1 (mod 3)?

1, 7, 13, 19, …   →   x ≡ 1 (mod 6)
x ≡ 1 (mod 6)  — that is, x = 1 + 6k.

Check: 1 is odd (≡ 1 mod 2) and 1 ≡ 4 (mod 3) since 4 ≡ 1. ✓

Make it a lesson. Always check the answer back against every original condition. Plug x = 4 into "x ≡ 1 (mod 2)" — it fails — so 4 can't be right.
